# Assessment — Carpal-Tunnel-Pattern Symptoms

## Reported context

- About 4 years of symptoms, fluctuating: sometimes better, sometimes worse.
- Wake up with both hands very numb ("asleep") in the morning.
- Heavy computer use during the day causes noticeable discomfort — described as
  bothersome, not painful.
- Push-ups with open, extended wrists cause wrist discomfort. This has improved a lot
  over time (it used to be much worse, now it's manageable but still present).
- Push-ups with closed fists (wrist kept closer to neutral) are noticeably more
  comfortable than open-hand push-ups.
- Two stated goals: (1) build hand/wrist/forearm muscle, and (2) improve circulation
  to the hands.

## Why this pattern fits carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS)

- **Nocturnal numbness is the single most classic CTS symptom.** During sleep the
  wrist tends to drift into flexion, and flexion is one of the two wrist positions
  (with extension) known to spike pressure inside the carpal tunnel and compress the
  median nerve. Hours in that position overnight is a very common trigger for
  waking up numb.
- **Keyboard/mouse use with wrists in extension or ulnar deviation** raises carpal
  tunnel pressure in a similar way, which lines up with the "worse with heavy
  computer use" pattern.
- **Open-palm push-ups load the wrist in deep extension** — again, one of the two
  positions that spikes carpal tunnel pressure the most. That directly explains the
  discomfort. **Closed-fist push-ups keep the wrist much closer to neutral**, which
  is exactly why they feel better. This single detail is a strong confirming signal
  for a mechanical, position-driven median nerve issue rather than something else.
- A multi-year *fluctuating* course — without progression to constant numbness,
  weakness, or thenar (thumb-pad) muscle wasting — is generally a reassuring sign.
  It points toward a mechanical/positional problem that responds to load and posture
  changes, rather than advanced nerve damage. That said, it isn't a substitute for
  an in-person exam.

## Recommended next step, in parallel with starting exercises

At some point it's worth getting a real baseline: a hand surgeon, orthopedist, or
physiotherapist can run Phalen's and Tinel's tests and, if useful, order a nerve
conduction study (EMG/NCS) to grade severity objectively. This doesn't have to
happen *before* starting — ergonomics, night positioning, and nerve gliding are safe
first moves — but it should happen if:

- numbness stops resolving after waking and moving the hands,
- there's new weakness or dropping objects,
- the thumb-pad muscle looks visibly smaller on one or both hands.

None of those have been reported. This is just the trigger list to watch for.

## Plan pillars to build out together (see the other files in this folder)

1. **Ergonomics** — keyboard/mouse position, wrist neutrality, break cadence.
   (`ergonomics.md`)
2. **Night positioning** — preventing wrist flexion during sleep, since that's the
   most likely driver of the morning numbness.
3. **Nerve gliding** — median nerve tendon-glide drills to reduce irritation.
4. **Strength** — balanced flexor/extensor and grip work, progressed carefully so it
   doesn't flare symptoms.
5. **Circulation** — warm-ups, contrast exposure, general movement/blood flow.
6. **Push-up wrist tolerance** — a specific progression to make open-hand push-ups
   fully comfortable again.

   (`exercise-plan.md` for 3–6)

Log how each of these actually affects mornings and computer-heavy days in
