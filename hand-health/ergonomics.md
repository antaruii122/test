# Ergonomics — DRAFT (to refine together)

Goal: reduce the two wrist positions that raise carpal tunnel pressure the most —
extension and flexion — during both computer use and sleep.

## Computer setup

- [ ] Keyboard and mouse at a height where wrists stay roughly **neutral/straight**,
      not bent up (extension) or down (flexion) while typing.
- [ ] No wrist rest used *while actively typing* — resting the wrist on a pad while
      typing tends to push it into extension. A wrist rest is fine for pauses
      between typing bursts, not during.
- [ ] Elbows roughly at ~90°, close to the body, not reaching forward/out for the
      keyboard or mouse.
- [ ] Mouse close enough that the shoulder doesn't need to reach — reaching pulls
      the wrist into awkward angles too.
- [ ] Consider a split or tented keyboard if extension/ulnar deviation is hard to
      avoid on a standard flat keyboard.
- [ ] Monitor height/distance set so the neck isn't compensating (posture upstream
      of the wrist matters too).

## Breaks

- [ ] Micro-breaks: brief hands-off-keyboard break every 20–30 minutes during heavy
      computer sessions (even 20–30 seconds of shaking out the hands / wrist circles
      helps).
- [ ] Longer break (a few minutes, away from the keyboard) roughly every hour.

## Night positioning

This is likely the biggest lever for the **morning numbness** specifically, since
sleep-time wrist flexion is a very common driver of it.

- [ ] Consider a night wrist splint that holds the wrist in neutral (not a rigid
      "cast" position — just prevents curling into flexion). This is one of the
      best-evidenced simple interventions for nocturnal CTS symptoms.
- [ ] Notice and adjust sleep position if hands end up tucked/curled under a pillow
      or the body.

## Daily habits

- [ ] Warm hands before a heavy computer session if they tend to feel stiff/cold
      first thing.
- [ ] Avoid gripping the mouse harder than necessary — a light grip reduces
      sustained flexor tension.
