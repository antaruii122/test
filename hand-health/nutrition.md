# Nutrition — DRAFT (to refine together)

Diet doesn't reverse structural nerve compression by itself, but several nutrients
and habits are linked to nerve health, inflammation, and fluid retention — all
relevant to carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS). This is a supporting lever, not a
replacement for ergonomics, night positioning, or a medical evaluation if needed.

## Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine)

- Low B6 status has been linked to CTS symptoms in some studies; B6 is a common
  supplement recommendation, but food-first is safer than high-dose supplements —
  very high-dose B6 supplementation can itself cause nerve problems, so more isn't
  automatically better.
- Food sources: chickpeas, poultry, tuna, salmon, potatoes, bananas, fortified
  cereals, spinach, sunflower seeds, avocado.

## Vitamin B12

- Supports nerve health broadly. Low B12 causes tingling/numbness on its own,
  independent of CTS, so it's worth having in the picture — especially on a mostly
  plant-based diet.
- Sources: eggs, dairy, meat, fish; fortified plant milks/cereals/nutritional yeast
  for vegetarians/vegans.

## Omega-3 fatty acids

- Anti-inflammatory; may help with general nerve/tissue inflammation (EPA/DHA in
  particular).
- Sources: fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), walnuts, flaxseed, chia seeds.

## Magnesium

- Involved in nerve and muscle function.
- Sources: nuts, seeds, leafy greens, whole grains, dark chocolate, legumes.

## General anti-inflammatory eating pattern

- **More of**: vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, olive oil, fatty
  fish. Spices like turmeric and ginger show up consistently in anti-inflammatory
  diet recommendations.
- **Less of**: ultra-processed food, excess added sugar, excess sodium — sodium
  matters here specifically because fluid retention can increase pressure inside
  the carpal tunnel (well documented in pregnancy-related CTS, and relevant in a
  smaller way generally).

## Hydration

- Adequate water intake supports tendon/connective tissue health and general
  circulation — relevant to both stated goals (muscle work and circulation).

## Weight and fluid retention

- Higher body weight and anything that causes fluid retention are independent risk
  factors for CTS — this is about physical space inside the carpal tunnel, not
  aesthetics.

## Caffeine / alcohol / sleep

- Both affect sleep quality. Since the morning numbness is tied closely to
  sleep-time wrist position and overall rest quality, keeping these moderate —
  especially close to bedtime — may help indirectly.

## What not to over-index on

No specific "carpal tunnel diet" is proven to reverse compression on its own.
Treat this list as background nutrition hygiene that supports the rest of the
plan, not a fix by itself.
